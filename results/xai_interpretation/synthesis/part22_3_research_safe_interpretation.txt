PART 22.3 — RESEARCH-SAFE XAI INTERPRETATION SYNTHESIS

MODEL
-----
Improved ConvNeXt-Tiny

SCOPE
-----
This interpretation is based on the deterministically selected 40-case XAI
cohort. It is descriptive and is not an unbiased population-level estimate.

The semantic annotations were obtained through structured visual review of
the locked Grad-CAM++ atlas and were not validated by an expert plant
pathologist or lesion-segmentation ground truth.

INTERPRETATION:
Within the selected external XAI cohort, wrong predictions were associated
with substantially more moderate/dominant background attention and lower
visible symptom-region alignment than correct predictions.

However, some external wrong predictions still showed STRONG/PARTIAL
symptom-region alignment. Therefore, external misclassification cannot be
explained solely by spatial mislocalization or background attention.
Incorrect disease discrimination can occur even when the network attends to
a visually abnormal leaf region.

that the external-domain performance loss is not adequately described as a
simple global failure of Grad-CAM localization across every PlantDoc image.

4. PREDICTED-CLASS vs TRUE-CLASS ATTENTION IN WRONG CASES
---------------------------------------------------------

External wrong CAM pairs:
17

4

Shift from off-leaf attention to relevant leaf/symptom tissue:

INTERPRETATION:
Changing the Grad-CAM target from the erroneous predicted class to the
ground-truth class altered the semantic attention pattern in multiple error
cases. In some cases, the true-class target moved attention toward visibly
abnormal tissue or away from background/context. This is consistent with
class-specific internal evidence being spatially different for competing
disease classes.

This pattern is not universal. Some true-class CAMs were unchanged or less
well aligned, so the evidence must not be presented as if the true-class CAM
always recovers the correct lesion.

INTERPRETATION:
The relatively low predicted-vs-true CAM similarity indicates that the
spatial evidence associated with competing classes is often substantially
different during misclassification. This supports the qualitative observation
that changing the target class can shift attention to a different image
region.


6. OVERALL XAI INTERPRETATION
-----------------------------

The selected XAI evidence is consistent with several simultaneous external
failure mechanisms:

1. Some external errors show increased dependence on background or scene
   context.

2. Some errors show weak or absent localization to visible symptomatic tissue.

3. Some errors remain symptom-focused but still predict the wrong disease,
   indicating that disease-class discrimination may fail even when spatial
   localization appears plausible.

4. Predicted-class and true-class CAMs often use different spatial evidence.

5. High predictive confidence does not guarantee correct or semantically
   appropriate attention under domain shift.


SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS
----------------------

These findings must be reported as descriptive XAI evidence.

They do NOT establish:

- causal background bias;
- causal disease reasoning;
- clinical diagnostic validity;
- lesion localization accuracy;
- population-level PlantDoc rates.

The XAI cohort was intentionally stratified and the semantic annotations were
not validated against expert pathology labels or lesion-segmentation masks.
